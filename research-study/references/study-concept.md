# Study: Concept Explanation

## Purpose

Explain a concept clearly, connect it to prerequisites, and verify the user can reproduce the key reasoning.

## Workflow

1. Identify the concept and the user's goal.
2. State the one-sentence definition.
3. Describe the motivating problem.
4. List assumptions and boundary conditions.
5. Explain the mechanism or derivation.
6. Provide a canonical example.
7. Provide a counterexample or failure case.
8. Connect to neighboring concepts.
9. Ask one retrieval question.
10. Ask one transfer problem.

## Quality gates

- The definition is precise and not circular.
- Assumptions and boundary conditions are explicit.
- The example is concrete and verifiable.
- The counterexample shows a failure mode.
- Neighboring concepts are linked, not listed.

## Common pitfalls

- Overloading the explanation with jargon.
- Skipping boundary conditions.
- Confusing correlation with causation.
- Treating a heuristic as a theorem.
- Hiding assumptions inside the derivation.
